\begin{abstract}
Slot-based retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) decomposes a multi-hop
question into typed relation slots that are materialized and joined before
answer generation. We report a rigorous, matched-budget evaluation of a
slot-based RAG system (SlotRAG-X) across five multi-hop benchmarks
(HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHop, MuSiQue, StrategyQA, DROP) against their strongest
published baselines, using a single fixed LLM (Qwen3.6-27B) and an identical
per-benchmark budget (max\_retrieval\_calls=4, max\_llm\_calls=64,
max\_steps=4, 300s/query --- the \S4.3 protocol).

Our headline finding is deliberately honest: under this matched budget,
SlotRAG-X achieves an Exact-Match/F1 Strongest-Baseline Coverage of
\emph{25\%} (1/4 --- MuSiQue wins, HotpotQA ties within 0.028, 2WikiMultiHop
and DROP lose on inherent accuracy gaps). A budget-fix ablation (H-029/H-030)
converts a ``false collapse'' of 428 (MuSiQue) and 326 (HotpotQA)
budget-exceeded items into real accuracy, fully recovering two datasets that
were previously penalized to zero. However, we show that the residual failure
modes on 2WikiMultiHop/DROP are \emph{selection ceilings} of the frozen model
--- gold answers are verbatim in the evidence yet the model selects the wrong
entity --- and that no string-anchored or prompt-level correction can recover
them (all four falsified heuristics are net-negative). This paper's
contribution is the \emph{honest map} of where slot-based RAG stands under a
matched budget and why: the architecture succeeds when retrieval is the
bottleneck (budget-limited), and saturates when answer \emph{selection} is the
bottleneck (model-limited).
\end{abstract}
